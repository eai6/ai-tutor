What stays where you put it

The database, uploaded media and the application itself stay wherever the country deploys them. Student records, session transcripts, results and teacher accounts do not leave that boundary.

What leaves, and why no setting changes it

When a student sends a message, that message is sent to the language model provider — Anthropic, OpenAI or Google — to be answered. It leaves the server, the cloud account and the country. This is not a configuration oversight; it is what the product is. Being explicit about it early is far cheaper than discovering it during a data protection review.

Also leaving the network, and worth listing so nobody is surprised by it in a firewall log: certificate issuance contacts Let's Encrypt, and container image pulls contact the image registry. Neither carries student data.

Three ways to close the gap

OptionWhat it gives youWhat it costs you
Regional provider endpointModel calls stay within a chosen jurisdiction, if the provider offers one thereAvailability varies by country; usually no quality loss
Self-hosted open modelNothing leaves your network at all. The platform speaks to Ollama, so an open model can serve the tutoring role while cloud models stay available for other purposesWeaker tutoring than a frontier model; hardware; slower replies
Offline desktop or kiosk buildThe model runs in the classroom. No network at allThe same quality trade-off, plus per-device hardware

The middle option is the one most often chosen for a national rollout, and the one most often chosen badly. Measure it on your own content before committing a cohort. How much weaker a local model is depends on the model, the hardware and the subject, and the platform ships the harnesses to find out (section 8).

Compliance features already in the platform

  • Consent records — consent granted and withdrawn, tracked per user.
  • Safety audit log — flagged content, rate limiting, age verification, authentication attempts, data exports and deletions, each with severity and a hashed user identifier so the log itself is not a second copy of the student register.
  • Subject access and erasure — data export and deletion routines for a student on request.
  • Age verification aligned to COPPA/GDPR expectations for minors.
  • Automated retention cleanup of data past its retention window.
  • Content flagging and teacher review of concerning student messages.
  • Hardened by external penetration testing — two assessment waves in 2026 remediated, covering input validation, login lockout, session and cookie handling, upload sandboxing and content security policy.

RECOMMENDATION

Run the data protection impact assessment in Phase 0, before content work begins, and make model placement its first finding. A country that decides in month four that model calls may not leave has to re-validate every subject already generated and reviewed.
