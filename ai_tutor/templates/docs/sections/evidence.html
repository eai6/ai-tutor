A ministry putting a language model in front of thirteen-year-olds needs to be able to answer two questions: how do you know it teaches well, and how do you know when it doesn't. The platform has three layers of answer.

Science of learning, built in

Five findings the lesson structure encodes, rather than leaves to the teacher.

Active learning
5E lesson structure: each step requires an active student response.
Scaffolding
The tutor provides hints, not answers, through sequenced teaching steps.
Mastery learning
Students advance by demonstrating understanding on the competency assessments.
Immediate feedback
The tutor identifies a student's errors and gives guidance in real time.
Retrieval practice
Concepts from earlier lessons are used as warm-up questions for later ones.

Layer 1 — The runtime judge

Every tutor reply is scored on ten dimensions before it is shown, in a single model call against a cross-vendor cascade. It catches factual errors, rule violations, incoherence and safety problems as they happen, and flags sessions for teacher review.

Layer 2 — Turn-level evaluation against a rubric

An evaluation workbench, built into the dashboard, annotates individual tutor turns from real production sessions against an internal rubric of thirty labels across nineteen failure categories. Both human annotators and model annotators score the same turns, so the two can be compared rather than assumed to agree.

Layer 3 — Session-level pedagogical evaluation

Whole sessions are scored against the eight pedagogical dimensions of the standard AI-tutor evaluation framework in the research literature (Maurya et al., NAACL 2025) — mistake identification, mistake location, revealing of the answer, guidance, actionability, coherence, tutor tone and human-likeness — on a three-way scale rather than pass/fail.

The quality gate to apply before a cohort

  • A sample of complete sessions per subject, hand-graded by a subject teacher against the session rubric — enough to see the failure patterns, not enough to be a research study.
  • A content review pass: every lesson in scope read and approved by a subject expert, with figures checked against the text they illustrate.
  • A language review pass by a native-speaking educator, on generated content and on live sessions, checking register and the absence of code-switching.
  • A safety walkthrough: deliberate off-topic, distressing and adversarial student messages, confirming flagging and teacher visibility work.
  • A cost measurement on the reviewed content, at the model configuration you intend to ship.

If a local model is being used instead of a cloud model, run this gate twice — once per configuration — and compare. That comparison is the evidence for the sovereignty trade-off, and it is the single most valuable artefact a country pilot can produce.
