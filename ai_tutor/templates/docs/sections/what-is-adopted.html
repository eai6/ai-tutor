For the student

A student signs in, picks a lesson from their grade's catalogue, and works through it in conversation. The session follows the 5E instructional model — Engage, Explore, Explain, Practice, Evaluate — with Socratic questioning and scaffolded hints rather than answers handed over. Lessons carry figures and diagrams. At the end an exit ticket draws ten questions from a bank of about thirty-five; eighty per cent is the pass mark, and the result is recorded as mastery of that lesson for that student.

Sessions can run on a shared classroom device with two students paired, or on individual accounts — a per-school setting, because device ratios differ by school, not by country.

For the teacher

Teachers get a dashboard that leads with triage: which students need attention, which lessons a class is failing, which tutoring sessions were flagged. They can review and edit generated lesson content, see per-student and per-class progress against competency bands, and read flagged transcripts. What teachers are allowed to do is configurable — editing content, uploading curriculum and regenerating courses are three independent permissions, all off by default, so a ministry can open them up at the pace it trusts.

For the ministry

Multi-tenancy is structural. Every query touching student data is scoped to an institution, with a platform-wide tier for content that all schools share. One national instance can therefore serve every school in the country while keeping each school's students and results separate, and a school's own uploaded material private to it.

The content pipeline

This is the part that makes national adoption tractable. A syllabus document goes in; a taught course comes out.

Everything after the parser is generated by a language model and is expected to be reviewed, not trusted. The review step is where a curriculum authority exercises its actual authority.

The quality layer

Every tutor reply is checked before it reaches the student by a unified judge — a single multi-axis model call scoring ten dimensions including factual accuracy, rule compliance, coherence, and safety. The judge runs against a cross-vendor cascade (Gemini, falling back to OpenAI, falling back to Claude Haiku), so a single provider outage degrades rather than stops the service.

WHAT THIS IS NOT

It is not a learning management system and does not replace a school information system — it integrates with them through an API. It is not a curriculum authority: it generates draft content that your subject experts approve or reject. It is not autonomous of teachers; the dashboard exists because the teacher is meant to intervene. And it is not a substitute for investment in devices and connectivity, though the offline builds lower the bar considerably.
