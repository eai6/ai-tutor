Every figure below is an observed cost from a 2026 deployment at pilot scale. Model pricing changes, cloud pricing differs by region, and usage per student is the largest single variable. Re-derive them for your country and your cohort before they enter a budget.

About US$58 of that — the NAT gateway, load balancer and firewall — is network safety rather than capacity. You pay it for a defensible network perimeter, not for the ability to serve one more student. A single-server deployment avoids all US$259 and accepts a single point of failure in exchange.

Variable cost — the model, and it dominates

Language model calls scale with teaching. In the Seychelles pilot they ran at roughly US$600 per month across four schools. This is the number that grows with a national rollout, and the number worth engineering against.

What drives it:

  • Tutoring turns per student per week — the primary driver. A student in three sessions a week costs three times a student in one.
  • Which model serves which purpose. The platform selects models per purpose — tutoring, judging, content generation, exit tickets — independently. Tutoring can run on a cheaper model than content generation without touching the rest.
  • The quality judge, which adds a second model call per tutor turn. It is a real cost and a real safeguard; it can be selectively sampled rather than run on every turn if budget requires.
  • Prompt caching. The stable prefix of the tutoring prompt is cached, so most of each turn's input is not re-billed at full rate. This is why the locale rules are written to be byte-stable — a careless edit to a locale string invalidates a country's entire cache.
  • Content generation, which is front-loaded per subject and then near zero until the syllabus changes.

Levers, in the order worth pulling them

LeverEffect

Modelling it for your country

A defensible per-student figure needs three numbers you already have or can decide: students in the cohort, sessions per student per week, and weeks in the school year. Multiply, then measure the cost of one full session on your own content — the platform's evaluation harness reports token usage and cost per run, so this is an afternoon's work rather than a guess. Do it in Phase 3, on reviewed content, not in Phase 0 on samples.
