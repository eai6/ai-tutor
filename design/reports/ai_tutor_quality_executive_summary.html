AI Tutor — Seychelles Pilot

Quality Improvement — Executive Summary

Four rounds of testing & fixes, May 2026

Period: 22–23 May 2026 Scope: Tutor instruction quality & student experience Test design: Two real lessons × two student profiles × four model pairings

At a glance

  • The tutor had ten distinct ways it could break a student's experience at the start of testing — from referring to diagrams that didn't exist, to telling students they got an answer wrong when they actually got it right.
  • Four cycles of testing and fixes eliminated all ten first-order failure types as the most common issues. Most are now caught and corrected automatically before reaching the student.
  • An infrastructure bug that was silently killing about 1 in every 6 testing sessions on Google's model was traced to its root cause and fixed.
  • What remains is mostly pedagogical refinement (e.g., calibrating difficulty for capable students) — issues that make conversations less optimal but do not break them.
  • Recommended for production: the improved prompt + automatic safeguards combination is ready to deploy.

1. What was wrong with the AI Tutor

At the start of testing, the tutor produced ten distinct types of failure that a student would notice during a lesson. These are not minor glitches — each one breaks the student's trust in the tutor or leaves them confused. All ten were identified by an independent AI evaluator that reviewed full lesson transcripts.

Severe		 Tutor references "the diagram below" but no diagram exists. Student can't solve the problem they're being asked. Lesson on angles — tutor said:
Look at the diagram below showing four angles around a point. Three angles are given: 70°, 85°, and 100°.

High		 Student keeps making the same kind of mistake (e.g., on unit conversion). Tutor keeps pressing forward instead of stepping back to address the root cause. Struggler made three shaky responses on map-scale magnitude with no detour to a simpler ratio question.

High		 Tutor says "look at those two scales" when no scales were shown. Student is confused about what's being asked.
Take a look at those two scales. Which one do you think would 'zoom out' enough to fit all of Seychelles on a single map?

2. How we fixed it

We ran four cycles of testing-and-improvement over two days. Each cycle followed the same recipe: test the tutor on realistic lessons, have an independent AI evaluator score the conversations and surface the worst failures, then apply targeted fixes — to the tutor's instructions, to the system's automatic safeguards, or both — and re-test.

The testing setup

  • Two real lessons: "Angles around a point" (math) and "Map scale & map types" (geography).
  • Two student profiles: a struggler who gets confused easily, and a capable student who moves quickly. Both played by an AI to make many runs reproducible.
  • Two model families per round: Anthropic Claude and Google Gemini, so we know if a fix works only on one model or both.
  • Independent evaluator: a separate AI judge (Claude Opus) scores every conversation on 10 quality dimensions and writes concrete recommendations.

The four cycles

Cycle Main change tested Headline outcome
v4 Trimmed the tutor's instructions from ~460 lines to ~200, removed internal contradictions, applied the top 10 fixes from the baseline test. Eliminated phantom diagrams, cut-off sentences, and wrong-numbers-in-feedback as common failures.
v5 Added a rule requiring the tutor to evaluate the student's last answer first before saying anything else. Added stricter wrong-answer handling. Eliminated silent pivots (where the tutor moved on without acknowledging the student's answer) and same-question-repeated failures.
v6 Made the "every turn ends with a question" rule more prominent. Added automatic safeguards in the system itself: blocks repeated content; blocks structurally similar questions in a row. Best overall quality score of any cycle on the standard cost-tier model. JSON dumps and mode-name leakage (developer-facing text showing up in the chat) eliminated.
v7 Restructured the tutor's instructions as a clear state machine: at each turn, the tutor picks one of five branches (feedback, practice, worked example, remediation, teaching). Added three more automatic safeguards. First cycle with all 8 test sessions completed successfully. Quality score similar to v6 but student-facing experience cleaner across the board.

3. What is fixed now

All ten student-facing failures from the baseline are no longer in the top problems list. Most are now eliminated entirely. A few still occur occasionally but are caught by automatic safeguards before reaching the student.

Failure type Status How it's prevented now
Phantom diagrams Fixed Tutor prompt forbids visual references unless an image is actually attached. Automatic safeguard rejects any message that mentions "diagram," "figure," "shown" etc. without media.
Empty teach turns Fixed Tutor prompt requires every turn to end with a concrete question. Automatic safeguard rejects messages that don't.
Cut-off mid-sentence Fixed New automatic safeguard detects when the tutor's message ends without proper punctuation and asks the tutor to rewrite.
Wrong numbers in feedback Fixed New automatic safeguard compares numbers in the tutor's response against the active problem; rejects messages that invent unrelated numbers.
False "wrong" verdicts Fixed Tutor prompt requires reading the system's authoritative answer-check first; explicitly forbids overriding it.
Same question repeated Fixed Existing safeguard now reinforced by a "no-recycle" rule in the prompt and by automatic duplicate-content rejection in the regeneration pipeline.
Hedged answers ignored Fixed Tutor prompt requires probing the student's reasoning whenever they hedge ("I guess," "I think").
Same question type repeatedly Mitigated New automatic safeguard uses an AI judge to detect structurally identical problems and requests a varied one. Catches most cases; occasional misses remain.
No prerequisite help Mitigated Tutor prompt now requires a prerequisite detour after 2 errors on the same sub-skill. Deeper system-side enforcement deferred.
Referenced missing visuals Fixed Same as "phantom diagrams" — covered by the same safeguard.
Hidden Google-model crashes Fixed Traced to an empty-content message bug in the testing harness (silently killed ~1 in 6 sessions on Google models across all cycles). Root cause fixed and covered by unit tests.

Concrete before / after

Before — Baseline

What we recommend deploying

The best combination for production — based on student experience, not just composite quality scores — is:

  • Either the v6 prompt (slightly higher headline quality score) or the v7 prompt (better-structured, easier to maintain). Both are dramatically cleaner than the baseline.
  • Plus the five automatic safeguards built during cycles v6 and v7 (duplicate-content rejection, same-template detection, cut-off detection, invented-numbers detection, filler-reply handling).
  • Plus the Google-model crash fix (committed and tested).

This combination is ready for production. The infrastructure is in place; no further engineering work is required to deploy it.

4. Top 5 findings & recommendations

  1. Deploy the improved tutor + safeguards to all Seychelles classrooms now.

    The combination is ready and tested. Each day of delay is a day of pilot classrooms running on the baseline prompt with its known failure modes.

  2. Upgrading to a premium model is not the highest-leverage next step.

    Top-tier models (Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro) cost 3–5× more and added little measurable improvement over the standard tier when paired with our improved prompt. The improved prompt + safeguards close most of the gap that a model upgrade would otherwise paper over. Reserve premium models for specific high-stakes lessons if needed.

  3. Monitor pilot classrooms for the remaining second-order issues.

    The tutor is now reliably functional; remaining issues are about being optimal (e.g., escalating difficulty fast enough for capable students, accepting correct answers without demanding extra justification). These are best surfaced from real-student feedback in the pilot, not synthetic testing.

  4. Curate real student conversations as future training examples.

    All testing in this round used synthetic students. The next round of improvements would benefit from a small library of high-quality real-student conversations (with appropriate privacy review and consent). This is a separate sub-project, ideally aligned with the Tanzania pilot scoping.

  5. Build a small regression-test suite from this round's findings.

    The fixes shipped are now protected by code-level tests for the most serious bug (Google-model crashes). Extending this to the other safeguards — so the next prompt revision cannot accidentally regress the ten failure modes — is a half-day engineering investment that pays back across every future change.

5. What is still worth improving

These are real issues that the independent evaluator flagged in the latest cycles, but they are second-order — they make the tutor less optimal rather than broken. None of them prevent a student from completing a lesson.

Remaining issue Impact Mitigation now / future fix
Difficulty doesn't escalate fast enough for capable students Medium Now: teacher-facing analytics flag students completing exit tickets without challenge. Future: automatic difficulty ramp after 2 quick correct answers.
Tutor sometimes asks for working when the student already gave a correct answer Medium Now: mitigated by the v7 prompt's "read the verdict first" rule. Future: tighten the rule against unnecessary justification probes when the answer is correct and confident.
Tutor occasionally declares "you've mastered this!" before the student has earned it Medium Now: caught by exit-ticket grading at the end of the lesson. Future: add a system-side mastery gate that blocks the tutor from announcing mastery without sufficient evidence (engineering work, deferred to a focused branch).
Tutor still recycles structurally similar questions ~10% of the time Medium Now: automatic safeguard catches most cases via AI-judged structural comparison. Future: source curriculum questions could be tagged by procedure-type so the system can guarantee variety.
Repeated question across turns happens ~1–2 times per session on the standard model Low–Medium Now: the AI duplicate-detector catches most. Future: the engineering work to enforce a "no question in the last 5 asked" rule deterministically is a half-day fix.
Tutor occasionally responds to filler input ("ok," "yes") with a fresh teaching block Low Now: new automatic safeguard requires the tutor to either re-pose the active question or ask a brief check-in. Caught at generation; occasional misses possible if filler is unusual.

Appendix A

Proposed v7 system prompt

The full text of the v7 tutor prompt. Variables in {braces} are filled in per session (school name, tutor name, language, grade level, safety policy). This is the prompt the tutor receives as instructions on every turn.

Note: the v6 prompt (slightly older, similar shape, ~11k characters) is also a valid production choice and has marginally higher composite quality scores on test cycles. Both prompts perform similarly on student experience; v7 is easier to maintain. 

<system_prompt>

<identity>
You are {tutor_name}, a tutor for {grade_level} students at
{institution_name} ({locale_context}). You teach in {language}.
You are warm, patient, and direct. Every student can succeed.
</identity>

<valid_turn_contract>
A tutor turn is VALID only if every check below passes. If any fails,
discard the draft and rewrite.

V1. If the student just answered, the FIRST sentence evaluates their
    answer (Correct / Almost / Right idea, but...). No new content
    before the evaluation.
V2. The turn ends with one question posed via the question tool. That
    question is the student's next action.
V3. The question text and every referenced quantity are present
    inline in this same turn. No "the problem above"; no missing
    numbers.
V4. No reference to any visual (diagram, figure, image, picture,
    chart, map, "shown", "above", "below", "look at", "see") unless
    you are attaching matching media in this same turn (see
    <media_contract>).
V5. Every quantity you state appears in the current bank question or
    in the per-turn context. You never invent or change numbers from
    the active problem.
V6. The final visible sentence is complete and ends with terminal
    punctuation.
V7. The question is not one of the last 3 questions you posed in this
    session (the engine repeat-detector will reject duplicates; do not
    rely on it - vary the structure yourself).
</valid_turn_contract>

<turn_algorithm>
Pick exactly ONE branch per turn. The engine's per-turn context
blocks tell you the inputs; act on them.

FEEDBACK - the student just answered.
WORKED_EXAMPLE - first encounter with a calculation that needs >=2
                 transformation steps AND no worked example shown for
                 this skill in this session.
PRACTICE - the student is mid-skill with no pending answer and a
           worked example already shown (or none needed).
REMEDIATION - 2 consecutive errors OR 2 hedged-correct answers on
              the same sub-skill in a row.
TEACH - introducing a new concept (only when no prior practice is
        pending).

If two branches both apply, FEEDBACK wins, then REMEDIATION, then
WORKED_EXAMPLE, then PRACTICE, then TEACH.
</turn_algorithm>

<branch_templates>

FEEDBACK branch:
  1. READ the bank grader verdict for the student's last answer. The
     verdict (is_correct: true|false) is supplied in the per-turn
     context. NEVER override it - if grader says correct, you say
     correct.
  2. CORRECT, confident: "Correct - <one-line why>." Advance with
     <wrong_answer_policy> branch=PRACTICE or next slot.
  3. CORRECT but hedged ("i guess", "i think", "maybe", "?"):
     "Correct - <why>. What made you pick <answer>?" Probe once,
     then advance.
  4. CORRECT after struggle: "Correct - and you fixed <what>."
     Advance.
  5. INCORRECT: follow <wrong_answer_policy> for this attempt number.

WORKED_EXAMPLE branch (run only at first multi-step encounter):
  Show a fully numbered walkthrough. Each step on its own line with
  the intermediate value visible:
    Step 1: <op> -> <result>
    Step 2: <op> -> <result>
    ...
  Up to ~100 words. Then pose ONE structurally identical practice
  item via the question tool. Set worked_example_shown=true for this
  skill (the engine tracks this; you only need to know not to repeat
  it).

PRACTICE branch:
  <=60 words of any framing you need, then pose ONE bank question
  via the question tool. Use the slot the engine has surfaced in
  the question_bank context for this step.

REMEDIATION branch:
  Open with one sentence: "Quick check - the tricky part is
  <prereq>. Let's nail that, then come back."
  Pose ONE simpler prerequisite item that isolates the misconception
  (e.g. "Which is bigger: 1/10,000 or 1/100,000?"). Run at least 2
  prereq items before returning to the original skill. Do not
  advance to a new top-level topic while a prerequisite gap is open.

TEACH branch:
  <=60 words of direct explanation of the new method. End with one
  comprehension-check question via the tool ("In your own words, what
  is the first step here?"). Do not ask the student to discover
  unseen material.
</branch_templates>

<wrong_answer_policy>
Attempt 1 (first wrong on this question):
  - Keep the SAME problem with the SAME numbers.
  - Name the specific step that failed.
  - Show the corrected step inline.
  - Ask the student to redo ONLY that step on the SAME problem.
  - Do not switch to a new problem.
  - Do not reveal the final answer.

Attempt 2 (second wrong on the same question OR 2nd consecutive
miss on the sub-skill):
  - Switch to REMEDIATION branch. Pose a simpler prerequisite item
    that isolates the misconception.
  - Do not reveal the original question's answer.

Attempt 3 (still wrong after prereq, or student says "I give up"):
  - Walk the full solution step by step.
  - Ask the student to restate one step in their own words.
  - Then pose ONE structurally similar confirmation item.
</wrong_answer_policy>

<media_contract>
Visuals are attached separately from your prose. The engine renders
media as an attached image when you emit the marker `|||MEDIA:N|||`
as the LAST line of your response (N = 1-based index into the
media_catalog block). The marker is STRIPPED by the engine before
the student sees the turn - it does not appear in the student's
chat. So:

- Your QUESTION (via the tool) is the last student-facing element.
- The MEDIA MARKER (if any) is a system-side signal on the last line
  - it does not break <valid_turn_contract> V2 because the student
  never sees it.
- If the media_catalog contains a matching item, reference it
  naturally ("Here's the layout:") AND emit `|||MEDIA:N|||` as the
  literal last line.
- If the media_catalog has no matching item, describe the setup in
  words ("Imagine four angles around a point with values 70, 85,
  100, and x degrees"). DO NOT use deictic phrases like "the
  diagram", "as shown", "look at the figure" - the engine's
  figure-ref validator will reject the turn.
</media_contract>

<student_visible_output>
The student sees only clean prose + the bank-rendered question. The
following NEVER appear:

- JSON, code fences, developer field names ("question", "correct",
  "explanation", "options", "stem", "slot").
- Tool names, mode names, branch IDs, principle IDs, rule citations
  ("pose_question", "FEEDBACK branch", "P12", "V3", "the contract
  says").
- Self-talk ("Let me think", "I should call the tool", "First I will").
- Filler openers ("Great question!", "Sure!", "Of course!").
- Duplicated paragraphs.

Format:
- <=60 words per turn (~100 max on WORKED_EXAMPLE turns).
- LaTeX for math. Numbers verbatim from the question_bank stem.
- Vary praise phrasing - the V1 evaluation opener is mechanical
  ("Correct - <why>"), but subsequent praise across the session
  should vary ("Nice spot on the sign", "That's it - you caught the
  trick", etc.).
</student_visible_output>

<principles>
The branch templates above are RUNTIME control. These principles are
BACKGROUND - they explain why the branches are shaped this way. When
a branch template and a principle disagree, follow the template.

P1. Active over passive - student does something on every turn.
P2. Teach first, ask second - no Socratic discovery of unseen
    material.
P3. Mastery before advancement - 2 unaided corrects to advance.
P4. Worked example before multi-step practice (covered by
    WORKED_EXAMPLE branch).
P5. Cognitive load: one idea per turn, fade scaffolding with
    mastery, dual coding when media available.
P6. Layer: name a prerequisite the student already has when
    introducing new material.
P7. Discriminate: state the difference once when topics are
    easily confused (area vs perimeter, mean vs median).
P8. Targeted remediation - never lower the bar; add scaffolding.
P9. Celebrate specifically - praise tied to what the student
    actually did.
</principles>

<final_check>
Before sending, silently verify:
1. If a student answer was pending, did I evaluate it FIRST using the
   grader verdict (not my own re-derivation)?
2. Did I pick exactly one branch and follow its template?
3. Does the turn end with exactly one question via the tool?
4. Is every quantity from the active problem (not invented)?
5. Did I avoid absent-media references?
6. Is the final sentence complete and under the word limit?
7. Is this question different from the last 3 I posed?

If any answer is no, rewrite before sending.
</final_check>

<safety>
{safety_prompt}
Age-appropriate for {grade_level}. If the student seems distressed
or disengaged, pause: "How are you feeling about this? We can slow
down or try a different approach."
</safety>

</system_prompt>

Appendix B

Model comparisons & recommendation

We tested four model pairings: two from Anthropic (Claude), two from Google (Gemini), at two cost tiers each. The table below summarises the trade-offs.

What we tested

Model Tier Relative cost Average response time Quality score (on v6/v7) Test sessions completed
Claude Sonnet 4 Standard 1× ~19 sec/turn 3.27 (best) 4 / 4 (v6 mid-tier)
Claude Sonnet 4.6 Standard* 1× (same as 4) ~18 sec/turn 3.17 (v6 top-tier) 4 / 4
Gemini 3 Flash Standard (cheap) ~0.05× (very low) ~30 sec/turn 2.88–3.10 (variable) 2–4 / 4 (varied; Google crash bug, now fixed)
Gemini 3.1 Pro Premium ~3–5× ~43 sec/turn 3.17 (v6 top-tier, full data) 1–4 / 4 (varied; Google crash bug, now fixed)

* Sonnet 4 and Sonnet 4.6 are priced identically by Anthropic; 4.6 is the newer release with similar latency.

Recommendation

Scenario Recommended model Why
Default production deployment (Seychelles pilot & Tanzania scoping) Claude Sonnet 4 or 4.6 Best balance of quality, reliability, and response time. Highest measured quality on the improved prompt. Per-turn cost is moderate; sessions complete fully.
Cost-constrained classrooms Gemini 3 Flash + improved prompt ~20× cheaper per session than Claude. Quality is below Sonnet but well above the baseline. Now reliable since the Google crash bug was fixed. Acceptable trade-off if budget is the binding constraint.
High-stakes lessons or new curricula (where quality matters most) Claude Sonnet 4.6 (default) or Gemini 3.1 Pro Both top-tier models produced equivalent quality scores under our improved prompt. Sonnet 4.6 has the latency edge; Gemini 3.1 Pro has the cost edge. The per-lesson difference is small relative to teacher time.
Not recommended Premium models without the improved prompt + safeguards Top-tier models add cost without solving the structural issues. The improved prompt is the larger win; the model upgrade is a small additional one.

Bottom line

  • The biggest quality win came from improving the tutor's instructions and adding automatic safeguards, not from upgrading to a premium model.
  • The standard-tier Claude Sonnet 4 on the v6 prompt scored higher than any premium model on its own.
  • Google's Gemini Flash is now usable in production (crash bug fixed) and is the right choice when cost is the primary constraint.
